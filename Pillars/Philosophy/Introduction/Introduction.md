# Introduction

## Overview

Introduction is the first of the three chapters of [[Knowledge Islands]], answering why the model exists before explaining how it works. It is followed by [[Model/Model|Model]], the portable definition any island can adopt, and then by [[Realisation/Realisation|Realisation]], where that definition becomes concrete in Arcadia. Introduction assumes no prior knowledge: it earns the idea of a Knowledge Island by showing what knowledge is, why it needs a home and what has been tried before, and then establishes the vocabulary the later chapters depend on.

The chapter has two parts, read in order. Background builds the problem and arrives at the island as its answer; Concept takes that single island and explains how islands relate, who acts within them, who holds authority and how change is governed. Together they give a reader enough context that nothing in the Model arrives without explanation.

---

## Background

[[Background/Background|Background]] makes the case for the model. Its notes move from an orienting entry point through the history of knowledge systems, the distinction between information, knowledge and wisdom, the scales at which knowledge lives and the prior art the model draws on, to the cycle of capture, connection and reflection and finally the island as the home of knowledge. Read it first: it is where the island, its Capital, Library, Streams and Harbour are introduced.

---

## Concept

[[Introduction/Concept/Concept|Concept]] supplies the foundational vocabulary: territories and archipelagos, agents, jurisdiction and governance, each a prerequisite for the next. Every concept is portable, with any Arcadia-specific instance held in `Admin/` rather than here. Its closing note on governance introduces the elements the Model then develops in full, so it is the natural handover into the next chapter.
