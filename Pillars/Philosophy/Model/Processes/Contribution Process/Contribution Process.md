# Contribution Process

## Overview

Arcadia holds the canonical public Knowledge Islands model. The Contribution Process distinguishes a receiving territory's adoption of published knowledge from a contribution to Arcadia's canonical sources. Both preserve the receiving owner's authority.

A public source can be read or adopted without the source keeping a reciprocal list of consumers. A restricted exchange or a request for another island's work requires its own authority and admission rules.

---

## Public adoption

A receiving island or territory may reference or adopt published KI knowledge under the source's stated publication conditions. It chooses the source revision, records provenance, decides whether to keep a reference or an adapted local copy, and owns its resulting knowledge and governance.

Updates are discovered and pulled when the receiver chooses to review them. Publication creates no automatic adoption, update or execution obligation. A private territory can keep its adoption declaration and local adaptations privately; it need not disclose its identity to Knowledge Islands or become a member of the KI territory.

Provenance identifies the source and known revision and distinguishes the original from a reference or adaptation. The applicable note format owns the metadata fields; a citation must remain useful when the source or its public projection changes.

---

## Contributions to the KI model

Anyone may identify a gap or offer an idea through a permitted intake. A formal proposal to change Arcadia's canonical model is carried by a member of its Council and follows Arcadia's Enactment Process. Citizenship and Council membership are recorded by the relevant island's Admin governance, and standing in another territory grants no automatic authority here.

A contribution must:

1. State the generic, portable knowledge offered and its source.
2. Establish authority to share the material, including its audience and any restrictions.
3. Enter the receiving process as draft work without assuming adoption or priority.
4. Receive Arcadia's review and acceptance before changing its canonical stores.

Knowledge specific to the contributor's territory stays under that territory's ownership unless a separately approved adaptation has a useful place in the KI model. A contribution offers knowledge; it does not grant the contributor write authority or oblige Arcadia to act.

---

## Internal routing and restricted exchange

Within a territory, shared governance may define routing conventions. Each island still decides whether material may enter its stores and whether a proposed change is accepted. Membership does not remove protected audiences, source restrictions or repository-owned approval.

Restricted exchange between territories requires the participating owners' explicit rules for purpose, audience, source permission, admission, adaptation and onward use. Shared citizenship, a company binding, an Agora or a checkout location is not sufficient permission. Protected source-store contents do not become public merely because their owning repository is public.

The territory owns its adopted exchange policy. Concrete repository routes and record lifecycle follow the applicable trade contract; transport implementation follows its product owner. Neither this process nor a declared route grants new implementation, release or publication authority.

---

## Practical distinctions

- **Public reference:** cite published knowledge and its source; the receiver owns its interpretation.
- **Public adoption:** retain a known revision and provenance; the receiver chooses adaptations and later updates.
- **Contribution:** offer authorised portable knowledge through the destination's intake; its owner decides acceptance.
- **Internal routing:** apply the territory's rules while retaining source access and destination acceptance boundaries.
- **Restricted exchange:** establish the participating owners' permission and audience rules before transfer.
- **Work request:** the receiving repository decides priority, planning, execution and acceptance.
