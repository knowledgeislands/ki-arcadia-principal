# Processes

## Overview

Processes are how governance is carried out in practice. Within the Model act, this chapter holds the active procedures that move knowledge from an idea to accepted content, as distinct from the conventions that describe what accepted content looks like. They are what give an island's council authority a concrete form.

The chapter has two processes facing in different directions. One governs change within an island; the other governs how knowledge passes between a territory and the shared Knowledge Islands model. Both preserve the authority of whoever owns the receiving knowledge.

Start with [[How Change Happens]], the narrative chapter note, before reading either process in detail.

---

## How Change Happens

[[How Change Happens]] introduces the chapter and explains why its contents are procedures rather than policies. It distinguishes the internal process from the external one and relates both to the island's governance and council. It is the orientation to read first, before the detail of each process.

---

## Enactment Process

[[Enactment Process/Enactment Process|Enactment Process]] is the internal mechanism through which a council exercises its authority over an island's canonical content. It sets out the philosophy of the gate that change must pass before reaching Pillars or Resources, and how proposals move between Streams and ratification. The operational mechanics are owned by the shared change-management skills; this note holds the governing intent and the local specifics.

---

## Contribution Process

[[Contribution Process]] distinguishes a territory's adoption of published Knowledge Islands knowledge from a contribution to Arcadia's canonical model. It explains how public adoption, formal contributions, and internal routing or restricted exchange each work, and why none of them transfers authority away from the receiving owner. Read it when bringing knowledge into an island from the shared model or offering knowledge back to it.
