# Conventions

## Overview

Conventions make an island coherent over time, so that any agent, human or AI, can navigate it reliably and contribute without ambiguity. Within the Model act, this chapter specifies the shared rules that every note, agent and tool working on an island should follow. Without them, notes accumulate inconsistently and knowledge becomes hard to find.

The chapter has three areas, each answering one question about a piece of knowledge. Notes answers what it should look like, Structure answers where it lives, and Residency answers whether it belongs on the island at all or in an agent's memory. The three are designed to be applied together rather than in isolation.

Start with [[What Conventions Cover]], the narrative chapter note, before turning to any one area.

## What Conventions Cover

[[What Conventions Cover]] introduces the chapter and explains why conventions matter to an island's usefulness. It frames the three areas as questions any piece of knowledge must answer and makes clear that conformance to all three is expected. It is the orientation to read first, before the detailed reference in each area.

## Notes

[[Notes]] defines the written form of island knowledge and why Markdown is a fundamental requirement of the model rather than a tooling preference. It gathers the conventions for note format, for frontmatter properties and tags, and for the specialised note types that extend the base format. It is the primary reference for anyone authoring or reviewing a note.

---

## Structure

[[Structure]] defines the island's geography: its physical stores, its governance infrastructure, and the zones of the Library, Streams and beyond. It sets out the top-level folders, the routing rules for new content, and the boundary between Pillars and Resources. Read it when navigating, extending or reorganising an island.

---

## Residency

[[Residency]] explains that knowledge on an island lives across tiers: the Library as the durable, authoritative store, and agent memory as a faster way to access it. It sets out what belongs in each tier, how knowledge moves between them, and the criteria for promoting a working observation into the Library. Read it when deciding whether something should be written to the island, held in memory, or discarded.
